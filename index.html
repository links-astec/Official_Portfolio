Research

My first paper, from my time at KNUST: a heart-disease screening model small enough to run on a microcontroller.

Journal article · IJSET Vol. 11, Issue 6 · 2023

Design of a TinyML-Based Predictive Heart Disease Screening Device

Acquah Andrews, Quaye Nii Attoh Gabriel — Computer Engineering, KNUST

A shallow neural network on an Arduino Nano 33 BLE Sense predicts heart failure from eight clinical features. It runs entirely on the device — no internet connection — which makes it usable in clinics with limited infrastructure.

82.61%accuracy
92.15%ROC-AUC

About

I'm an AI engineer from Ghana, living in Tourcoing, France. I'm doing an engineering degree in Artificial Intelligence at JUNIA ISEN, and most of my work sits where models meet products: retrieval systems, LLM evaluation, and the APIs and interfaces around them.

Since September 2026 I've been a data / AI engineer in alternance at OXYA. Before that I spent the summer as a research intern at Sheffield Hallam University working on LLM security, and two years as CTO of GLINAX in Ghana.

I care about the unglamorous parts — chunking, evaluation, deployment — because that's usually where a model succeeds or fails.
